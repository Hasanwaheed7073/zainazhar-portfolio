🔴 Critical Issues

1. No custom domain — running on vercel.app subdomain
Critical

The site lives at zainazhar.vercel.app. This is the single biggest SEO handicap. Vercel subdomains carry almost no domain authority. Google treats them as generic user-generated pages. A custom domain like zainazhar.com or zainazhar.io would consolidate all ranking signals to a domain you own and control.

Fix Buy zainazhar.com (or zainreverserecruiter.com as a keyword-rich option), add it in Vercel's Domains settings, then update all canonicals, sitemap URLs, og:url, og:image URLs, and the LinkedIn portfolio URL. Also add a 301 redirect from the vercel.app URL.
2. Interview count inconsistency — 3,400 on LinkedIn vs 400 on website
Critical

LinkedIn headline says "3400+ Interviews Facilitated". The website, blog posts, meta descriptions, and About section all say "400+ interviews". Google's E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) algorithm cross-references your claims. A 10x discrepancy erodes trust and could flag the site as inconsistent or exaggerated.

Fix Decide on the real number and make it consistent everywhere: homepage hero stats, About section, meta description, every blog post reference, LinkedIn headline, and LinkedIn About. If 3,400 is accurate, update all website references. If 400 is accurate, fix LinkedIn.
3. Hero counter stats render as "0+" for search engines
Critical

The homepage and /job-seekers hero section shows animated counters: "0+ Clients Supported", "0+ Interviews Facilitated", "0+ Years Executing". These are JavaScript-animated — the initial server-rendered HTML contains "0" as the starting value, which is what search engine crawlers read. Googlebot does execute JS but these animations typically start at 0 and count up, so the crawl snapshot often captures the zero state.

Fix — set the real number as the default HTML value, not 0 
<!-- Before (server-renders as 0, JS animates up) -->
<span data-count="300">0</span>+

<!-- After (server-renders the real number, JS can still animate) -->
<span data-count="300">300</span>+
If using a counter animation library, set the default slot/initial value to the target number so crawlers always see the real stat.

5. LinkedIn URL is a random hash — not a custom URL
Critical

linkedin.com/in/zain-a-385a0b236 — the 385a0b236 suffix is a generated ID. LinkedIn custom URLs rank better in Google for your name-based searches. This URL also appears on your website and in your meta tags, meaning any visitor or crawler following it sees a non-descriptive URL. It's also harder for people to type or remember.

Fix Go to LinkedIn → Me → View Profile → Edit Public Profile & URL (top right) → set to linkedin.com/in/zainazhar or linkedin.com/in/zain-azhar. Then update the URL everywhere on your website (footer, contact section, metadata).

🟠 High Priority

6. Twitter/X meta tags are not page-specific
High

Every page — /job-seekers, /blog, and all blog posts — uses the homepage's twitter:title ("Zain Azhar — Reverse Recruiter for Career Coaches") and twitter:description ("Job search execution partner for career coaches. 300+ clients, 400+ interviews"). When someone shares your /job-seekers or a blog post on Twitter/X, the preview card shows the wrong context. It also signals to social crawlers that these are duplicate pages.

Fix — make twitter: tags match the page title and og: tags 
<!-- /job-seekers -->
<meta name="twitter:title" content="Reverse Recruiter for Job Seekers — Zain Azhar" />
<meta name="twitter:description" content="A done-for-you job search. Daily applications, outreach, follow-ups for US, UK, and Canada professionals." />

<!-- /blog post -->
<meta name="twitter:title" content="What Is Reverse Recruiting? A Plain Guide" />
<meta name="twitter:description" content="Reverse recruiting is a done-for-you job search run by a specialist. Here is how it works and who it is for." />
7. Blog index page missing og:image
High

The /blog page has no og:image tag at all. When shared on social media, no preview image will render — just title and description on a blank card. This significantly reduces click-through rate from social shares.

Fix Add an og:image to the /blog page. Use the same opengraph image as the homepage, or create a blog-specific banner. 
<meta property="og:image" content="https://zainazhar.vercel.app/opengraph-image" />
<meta property="og:image:alt" content="Reverse Recruiting Blog — Zain Azhar" />
<meta property="og:image:width" content="1200" />
<meta property="og:image:height" content="630" />
8. Blog posts missing article:published_time and article:author OG tags
High

Your blog posts set og:type = article correctly but don't include the matching article-type Open Graph tags. Google uses article:published_time to assess freshness and article:author to attribute content to your Person entity. Missing these weakens your blog's topical authority signals.

Fix — add to each blog post's <head> 
<meta property="article:author" content="Zain Azhar" />
<meta property="article:published_time" content="2025-09-01T00:00:00+00:00" />
<meta property="article:modified_time" content="2026-07-01T00:00:00+00:00" />
<meta property="article:section" content="Career Services" />
<meta property="article:tag" content="reverse recruiting" />
9. format-detection meta tag blocking phone/email on mobile
High

meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, address=no, email=no" tells mobile browsers NOT to auto-detect and link your phone numbers and email addresses. Your main CTAs are WhatsApp (+92 308 7823424) and email — disabling auto-detection means mobile users can't tap to call or email directly from un-linked text. This hurts mobile conversion.

Fix Remove the format-detection meta tag entirely, or change it to allow phone and email detection: 
<!-- Remove this line entirely -->
<meta name="format-detection" content="telephone=no, address=no, email=no" />

<!-- Your WhatsApp and email links are already properly wrapped in <a href="tel:">
     and <a href="mailto:"> so auto-detection adds a useful fallback -->
10. External links missing rel="noopener noreferrer"
High

Links to Google Sheets trackers, Google Docs (offer letters, interview records), and external services open in a new tab but lack rel="noopener noreferrer". This is both a security issue (tab-napping vulnerability) and a minor PageRank consideration.

Fix — add to all external links that use target="_blank" 
<a href="https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/..."
   target="_blank"
   rel="noopener noreferrer">Open →</a>
In Next.js, if using the Link component for external URLs, add rel="noopener noreferrer" as a prop.
11. LinkedIn profile sections — low public visibility
High

From the public-facing profile, only your headline and location are visible without login. The About section, experience details, and skills are collapsed or hidden. LinkedIn's SEO works through keyword-rich text in your About, Experience, and Skills sections — if these are thin or set to private, you won't rank in LinkedIn search results or Google searches like "reverse recruiter Albany NY".

Fix Settings & Privacy → Visibility → Edit your public profile → make About, Experience, Skills, and Recommendations all visible to "Everyone". Then ensure your About section opens with a keyword-rich first two lines (these appear in Google's snippet without truncation). 
Suggested About opening:
"Reverse Recruiter based in Albany, NY. I run end-to-end job search execution
for career coaches as a white-label execution partner — applications, recruiter
outreach, follow-ups, and weekly reporting — across the US, UK, and Canada.
300+ clients supported. 3,400+ interviews facilitated in 5 years."

While Google doesn't directly use the keywords meta tag as a ranking factor, having identical keyword meta signals across pages is a symptom of insufficient page differentiation — and the underlying SEO targeting should be different for each audience.

<meta name="keywords" content="reverse recruiter for job seekers,done-for-you job search,
managed job search USA,job search execution service,ATS resume optimization,
recruiter outreach service,job search outsourcing,reverse recruiting for professionals" />
13. robots.txt uses non-standard "Host:" directive
Medium

Your robots.txt includes Host: https://zainazhar.vercel.app. This directive is only recognized by Yandex (Russian search engine) — Google, Bing, and others ignore it entirely. It doesn't hurt, but it's noise and adds confusion when debugging crawl issues.

Fix — simplify your robots.txt 
User-agent: *
Allow: /

Sitemap: https://zainazhar.vercel.app/sitemap.xml
14. Professional email not used — Gmail address as primary contact
Medium

The contact email throughout the site is zeecareers07@gmail.com. For a professional B2B service targeting career coaches, a Gmail address reduces perceived authority. When you move to a custom domain, you should set up zain@[yourdomain].com. Google Workspace costs ~$6/month.

Fix After purchasing a custom domain, set up Google Workspace or Zoho Mail and update the email in: footer, contact section, mailto: links, meta tags, and LinkedIn contact info.

16. Google Search Console not verified (cannot confirm)
Medium

There's no visible Google Search Console verification meta tag or DNS record in the fetched pages. If GSC isn't set up, you have no visibility into which queries drive traffic, what your click-through rates are, or whether Google is crawling all your pages. It's a free tool that's essential for SEO management.

Fix Go to search.google.com/search-console, add your property, verify via the HTML meta tag method, then submit your sitemap (https://zainazhar.vercel.app/sitemap.xml). Also set up Google Analytics 4 if not already running.
